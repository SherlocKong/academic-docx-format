# Scientific Formatting Rules

Apply the supplied journal or school template first. Use these rules only where the template is silent.

## Equations and inline notation

- Keep display equations editable, normally as Word OMML.
- Audit equation body, placement, numbering, and every textual reference together.
- Use Word character formatting for inline variables rather than plain-text underscores or caret notation.
- Italicize scalar variables and conventional mathematical symbols.
- Keep units, mathematical functions, element symbols, and descriptive subscripts upright roman.
- Use real subscript and superscript formatting.
- Keep equation numbering continuous unless the required template specifies section-based numbering.

## Units

- Keep numbers and units upright.
- Use consistent spacing between values and units according to the target style.
- Use true superscripts for powers and negative exponents.
- Distinguish multiplication signs, minus signs, hyphens, and en dashes.
- Do not perform a global unit cleanup during a narrower task unless approved.

## Tables

- Classify data tables, equation-layout tables, and layout tables before editing.
- Derive border, header, alignment, width, and cross-page rules from the target template.
- Preserve table text, widths, heights, and local formatting unless approved.
- Check captions, numbering, and textual references together.

## Figures and captions

- Preserve image relationships, crop settings, sizing, and placement unless in scope.
- Check caption wording, numbering, style, and references.
- Verify images and captions after rendering.

## Fields, citations, and references

- Inspect fields and bookmarks before changing visible numbers.
- Separate reference-content correctness from reference-format correctness.
- Verify body citation coverage and reference-list continuity after edits.
- Do not mechanically rewrite personal names, organization names, compound surnames, or accented characters.
